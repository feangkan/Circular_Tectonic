## Studio Rationale

Circular Tectonics positions architecture within the urgent transition towards a circular built environment, where material reuse, digital technologies, and collaborative modes of construction are reshaping architectural practice. In response to increasing construction waste, resource depletion, and the environmental impacts of conventional building methods, the studio reconsiders tectonics through the lens of **circular material systems and computational design**.

Rather than viewing reclaimed materials as constraints, Circular Tectonics treats them as **active generators of architectural form, structural logic, and construction strategy**. Students investigate how irregular material stocks can be digitally catalogued, analysed, and transformed through computational workflows integrating:

- 3D scanning
- Parametric modelling
- Structural optimisation
- Digital fabrication
- Mixed-reality-assisted assembly

Computation is understood not simply as a tool for formal expression, but as a means of negotiating relationships between **material availability, fabrication constraints, structural performance, and architectural intention**.

The studio explores architecture as a **collaborative and socially engaged practice** through an international design-build partnership between RMIT University and The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK).

## The Studio Project — CRITICAL BRIEF DETAIL

### Project Description

> Individual pavilion-scale architectural intervention within a **notional full-scale spatial envelope of 3 m × 3 m × 3 m**.

This is the core design constraint for my individual proposal. It enables focused experimentation with:
- Structural systems
- Material organisation
- Spatial experience
- Fabrication processes
- Assembly strategies

**Two Outcomes:**
1. **Individual:** A resolved architectural proposal + scaled physical prototype (3D printing, CNC, laser cutting), fully authored by me
2. **Collective:** Contribution to a 1:1 full-scale installation, built collaboratively with CUHK students during the Hong Kong workshop, using **locally sourced reclaimed materials**

**Research-through-making approach:** Design development is continuously informed by the actual characteristics and availability of reclaimed materials — not the other way around.

## Five-Phase Studio Structure

### Phase 1 — Circular Thinking & Material Exploration (Weeks 2–3)
Precedent studies, material investigations, introductory computational workflows.
- Introduction to Circular Tectonics and circular design principles
- Precedent research into reclaimed material systems
- Material sourcing, classification, digital catalogue development
- Introduction to **Rhino, Grasshopper, Ameba, 3D scanning, Fologram**
- Initial design speculation
- **Milestone: Concept Proposal Pin-Up (Week 3)**

### Phase 2 — Computational Design & Prototype Development (Weeks 4–8)
Individual proposals developed within the 3×3×3m envelope via iterative computational workflows.
- Learning software (Weeks 4–6)
- Parametric design development
- Material-informed form generation
- Structural optimisation and tectonic exploration
- Connector and joint development
- Digital fabrication testing + physical prototyping
- **Milestone: Mid-Semester Review (Week 8, Wed 16 September)**

### Phase 3 — Design Resolution & Fabrication Planning (Weeks 9–13)
Refinement toward resolved architectural, material, structural, and fabrication outcomes.
- Design refinement and spatial development
- Structural/tectonic resolution
- Material and fabrication testing
- Connector and joint development
- Scaled model production
- Assembly sequence development
- Collaborative planning for the HK installation
- **Milestone: Pre-Travel Review (Week 13, Wed 21 October)**

### Phase 4 — Hong Kong Intensive Design-Build Workshop (Weeks 14–15)
At CUHK School of Architecture. RMIT + CUHK students collaborate on a **shared 1:1 installation** (not individual builds) using locally sourced reclaimed materials.
- Reclaimed-material assessment and preparation
- Digital fabrication and component production
- Connector and assembly development
- 1:1 installation fabrication and assembly
- Mixed-reality-assisted construction
- Academic exchange, architectural visits, cultural engagement
- **Milestone: Final Presentation at CUHK + Folio Submission (Mon 2 Nov); CUHK Exhibition (Tue 3 Nov)**

### Phase 5 — RMIT Exhibition, Documentation & Reflection (Late Week 15 & Week 16)
- RMIT Exhibition Setup — Friday 6 November
- RMIT Exhibition Opening — Friday 13 November

## Studio Conceptual Agenda — Guiding Questions

1. How can reclaimed materials become **generators** of architectural form and tectonic logic (not constraints)?
2. How can computational design **mediate** between material availability, fabrication, and construction?
3. How might collaborative design-build processes **redefine** contemporary architectural practice within a circular economy?
