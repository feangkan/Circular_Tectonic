# Key Concepts & Glossary

## Core Concepts

### Circular Economy
An economic system aimed at eliminating waste and the continual use of resources through cycling products and materials back into production. In architecture: design-for-disassembly, material reuse, cradle-to-cradle thinking.

**Relevant:** Design-for-reuse workflow, material cataloguing, end-of-life planning

### Circular Tectonics
This studio's framework: using **material reuse not as a constraint, but as a generator of form, structure, and construction logic**. Material availability becomes the design driver rather than a limitation.

**Relevant:** Form-finding from material constraints, tectonic expression through assembly, logic-driven design

### Tectonics
The study of how buildings are constructed and assembled. The expression of structural logic through material joints, connections, and assembly sequences. How design becomes visible through building.

**Relevant:** Connection design, tectonic expression, structural honesty, fabrication-aware aesthetics

### Design for Reuse (vs Design for Disassembly)
- **Design for Disassembly:** Breaking buildings apart for material recovery at end-of-life
- **Design for Reuse:** Creating components that can be removed, adapted, and reassembled without degradation
- *This studio emphasizes reuse*: components maintain integrity through new applications

## Studio-Specific Terminology

### Material Stock
The available inventory of reclaimed structural components, aggregates, and materials available for the pavilion design. Drives design methodology—you design *from* the materials, not *for* them.

### Material Cataloguing
Systematic documentation of reclaimed materials including:
- Physical properties (dimensions, material type, condition)
- Structural properties (strength, durability)
- Geometric variation (deviation from standard forms)
- Spatial organization (where pieces are located in warehouse/storage)

**Tools:** Spreadsheets, 3D databases, taxonomy systems

### 3D Scanning & Classification
Converting physical reclaimed components into digital data:
- **3D Scanning:** Photogrammetry or laser scanning to capture geometry
- **Classification:** Organizing point clouds by material type, size, condition
- **Segmentation:** Identifying usable vs damaged sections
- **Cataloguing:** Adding to material database for design reference

### Computational Form-Finding
Using algorithms and simulation to generate forms that:
- Respond to available material stock (shape, size, quantity)
- Optimize for structural performance
- Minimize material waste
- Express circular logic through design

**Tools:** Grasshopper, Python, Rhino scripts, optimization plugins

### Connector Design
Custom joints that enable reclaimed components (non-standard sizes/shapes) to work together:
- **Flexible connectors:** Allow accommodation of dimensional variation
- **3D-printed connectors:** Custom geometry for each connection point
- **Mixed-material logic:** Joining different material types (timber, steel, concrete, etc.)

### Mixed-Reality (MR) Assembly Guidance
Augmented reality instructions overlaid on physical space:
- Step-by-step assembly sequence
- Component positioning & orientation
- Connection details displayed in real-time
- Quality control and alignment checking

## Fabrication & Technical Terms

### GFRC (Glass Fiber Reinforced Concrete)
Composite material combining:
- Concrete matrix (cement, sand, water, aggregates)
- Glass fiber reinforcement (typically 5-10% by volume)
- Advantages: lightweight, formable, durable, customizable finishes
- Disadvantages: brittleness compared to steel, fiber orientation sensitivity

**Studio relevance:** Exploring GFRC as primary façade/component material

### Timber Hybrid Systems
Combining timber with other materials:
- **Timber + GFRC:** Structural timber frame with GFRC cladding/connections
- **Timber + Steel:** Hybrid structures where timber is primary, steel for critical joints
- **Timber + 3D-printed connectors:** Digitally-optimized connections for non-standard components

### Reclaimed Structural Components
Building components recovered from demolished structures:
- Structural steel sections (I-beams, angles, tubes)
- Timber beams and posts
- Concrete segments
- Architectural salvage (panels, trim, cladding)
- Advantages: embodied carbon reduction, character/patina
- Challenges: non-standard sizing, variable quality, irregular geometry

### Digital Fabrication
Computer-controlled manufacturing processes:
- **3D Printing:** Additive fabrication for connectors, joints, custom components
- **CNC Machining:** Subtractive for precision timber/material work
- **Laser Cutting:** Cutting and engraving for assembly guides, signage

### Design-for-Fabrication
Considering fabrication constraints during design phase:
- Tolerance requirements (±accuracy)
- Material removal rates (CNC speeds)
- Print settings (3D printer resolution, support structures)
- Assembly sequencing (what can be built first)

## Research Methodologies

### Material Testing
Systematic evaluation of material properties:
- **Structural Testing:** Load testing, compression, tension, shear
- **Durability Testing:** Weathering, UV exposure, water resistance
- **Fabrication Testing:** How materials respond to cutting, joining, finishing

### Precedent Analysis
Systematic study of relevant projects/practices:
- Project overview and design intent
- Material + tectonic strategies employed
- Fabrication methods used
- Outcomes and lessons applicable to this research

### Case Study Methodology
Deep investigation of 1-2 exemplar projects:
- Historical context
- Design development process
- Construction methodology
- Performance data (if available)
- Critical reflection on successes/challenges

## Sustainability Terms

### Embodied Carbon
The carbon emissions associated with material extraction, manufacturing, and transportation (before the building is used). Reclaimed materials reduce embodied carbon vs virgin materials.

### Lifecycle Assessment (LCA)
Analysis of environmental impact across a product's full lifecycle:
- Material extraction
- Manufacturing
- Transportation
- Use phase
- End-of-life/disposal or reuse

### Circular Economy Principles
- **Reduce:** Minimize new material consumption
- **Reuse:** Keep components in service through multiple iterations
- **Recycle:** Process materials at end-of-life into new products
- **Regenerate:** Return nutrients to natural/technical cycles

## Design Process Terms

### Iteration
Repeating design cycle: design → test/build → learn → refine. Core to this studio.

### Design-Build
Combining design development with actual construction/assembly. Happens in Hong Kong Phase.

### Prototype
Physical test model to validate:
- Structural performance
- Fabrication feasibility
- Assembly logic
- Aesthetic/tactile qualities

### Mock-up
Full-scale or detailed section test to validate:
- Assembly instructions
- Connection performance
- Material quality
- Installation sequencing

## Project Outcomes Terminology

### Pavilion
Lightweight, temporary (or semi-permanent) structure. Primary design output for this studio. Characteristics:
- Demountable/disassemblable
- Component-based assembly
- Responsive to context
- Built from reclaimed materials

### Installation
The final built outcome occupying CUHK campus. Documentation-intensive phase emphasizing process + result.
