# Week 3 (v2) — Initial Pavilion Exploration (3×3×3m)

Four directions, all led by the growth-algorithm/biomorphic computational approach, each testing a different material-and-fabrication answer to the same generative geometry. Deliberately unresolved, per the brief.

---

## Concept A — "Grown Skin" (differential-growth metal panelisation, Thin-Shell logic)

**Design concept:** A differential-growth simulation runs across a target double-curved surface, producing a stripe/panel segmentation pattern directly modelled on the Zephyr Pavilion/Thin-Shell Nest Pavilion method — panels rotated so each one carries load and connects to its neighbour simultaneously.

**Material strategy:** Reclaimed metal roofing sheet (M01) folded/bent per panel, riveted at seams — no separate frame, structure and skin are the same layer, exactly per the main precedent.

**Initial tectonic thinking:** Assembly sequence becomes a design constraint, not an afterthought — panels must go in a specific order (as in Zephyr), which itself could generate a visible construction "grain" across the surface.

**Strengths:** Most direct match to the strongest precedent researched; hand-tool achievable (fold/bend, rivet) before Hong Kong fabrication access opens.
**Open weakness:** Reclaimed sheet's variable condition/gauge may not tolerate the tight folding tolerances the precedent's new material achieved — needs a mock-up panel test.

---

## Concept B — "Cast Growth" (GFRC panels from a growth-generated flexible mould)

**Design concept:** The same differential-growth segmentation as Concept A, but realised in cast GFRC (basalt-fibre mix, M04) using a digitally-reconfigurable flexible mould table per the Thinking Space/Testing Space precedent — one mould, reshaped between every panel.

**Material strategy:** Basalt-fibre GFRC for the skin (avoiding the thin reclaimed-GFRC salvage stream entirely), with connection points (bolted or cast-in per the precedent's three tested types) designed into the mould from the start.

**Initial tectonic thinking:** A monolithic-vs-discretised comparison, exactly as the precedent ran — likely settling on discretised despite the deflection penalty, since a monolithic 3×3×3m cast piece isn't transportable or buildable at student scale anyway.

**Strengths:** Most rigorous structural precedent of all four concepts (real FEM numbers to reference); genuinely novel material story (basalt fibre swap).
**Open weakness:** No confirmed flexible-mould-table access — this is a fabrication method, not just a material choice, and needs equipment access confirmed before committing.

---

## Concept C — "Graded Frame, Standardised Joint" (timber, hybrid Re-SPLAM/Sanchez strategy)

**Design concept:** A primary frame built from reclaimed structural timber and CLT offcuts (M05/M06), using EPFL Ski Pavilion-style property grading for the few high-value pieces, combined with Sanchez/Xu et al.'s discrete mortise-tenon joinery — standardised connection rules — for the bulk of variable, lower-grade stock.

**Material strategy:** Two-tier — graded pieces at high-load nodes, standardised-joint pieces everywhere else, directly testing whether "grade the material" and "standardise the joint" can coexist in one structure rather than being two competing strategies.

**Initial tectonic thinking:** Growth-algorithm involvement here is different from A/B — instead of generating a skin pattern, it could generate the *voxel/rule sequence* for the discrete timber assembly (per Xu et al.'s method), making growth-logic and discrete-joinery-logic work together rather than as alternatives.

**Strengths:** Only concept directly testing the "two computational paradigms" question raised in the precedent research; lowest material cost.
**Open weakness:** Conceptually the most complex of the four — risks trying to prove too much in one pavilion; may need to simplify to just one strategy before Week 4.

---

## Concept D — "Layered Hierarchy" (all three materials, function-assigned)

**Design concept:** Direct continuation of the Blue Ocean Dome principle from earlier research — metal (M02) as primary tension/frame elements, GFRC (M04) as base/plinth or high-durability accent panels, wood (M05/M06) as bulk infill/skin — each material doing only the job it's genuinely good at.

**Material strategy:** Explicitly a hierarchy, not a mix — the point is to avoid forcing any one material (especially the thin GFRC stream) to do more than its realistic sourcing volume allows.

**Initial tectonic thinking:** Connections between different materials (metal-to-wood, wood-to-GFRC) become the most interesting design problem — likely the least resolved of the four concepts computationally, since the growth-algorithm lead applies most naturally within a single material system.

**Strengths:** Most realistic against actual sourcing constraints (spreads risk across three streams rather than betting on one); clearest "story" of genuine circular-material hierarchy for the presentation.
**Open weakness:** Cross-material connection detailing is unresearched — would need dedicated Week 4 work before this concept could be taken further.

## Comparison for Discussion

| | A: Grown Skin | B: Cast Growth | C: Graded Frame | D: Layered Hierarchy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary material | Metal sheet | GFRC (basalt) | Timber (mixed) | All three, function-split |
| Precedent strength | Strongest (Thin-Shell) | Strongest data (GFRC) | Strong but complex | Conceptual only |
| Fabrication dependency | Low (hand tools) | High (flexible mould access) | Medium | Medium-high (multi-material joints) |
| Computational role | Skin segmentation | Panel + mould geometry | Voxel/rule sequence | Least defined |
| Sourcing risk | Low | Medium | Low | Spread across 3 (lower risk, more coordination) |

No direction is committed — Wednesday's pin-up should surface which of these (or what combination) the studio feedback favours before Phase 2 computational work begins.
