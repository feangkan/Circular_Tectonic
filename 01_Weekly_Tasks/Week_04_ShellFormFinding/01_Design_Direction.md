# Week 4 — Design Direction: Shell / Hybrid-Shell Pavilion, Biomorphic Method

**Studio Week:** 4
**Responds to:** `00_Tutor_Feedback.md`
**Materials:** Reclaimed metal sheets + fabric (narrowed scope, confirmed by tutor)

---

## 1. Structure Type

**Shell or hybrid-shell structure**, within the standard 3m × 3m × 3m pavilion envelope.

- Pure shell: metal sheet skin is both envelope and primary structure (form-found for compression/tension, not bending).
- Hybrid shell: a lighter primary frame (from reclaimed structural sections, if needed for the 3D constraints) with the metal-sheet shell doing the enclosure and most of the spanning work, fabric filling secondary roles.

Which of the two depends on what the form-finding produces — see `02_Computational_Strategy.md`. Structural honesty matters here because sheet metal is weak in bending; the shell shape has to emerge from a force-based process, not be sculpted arbitrarily.

## 2. Method: Biomorphic (Neri Oxman lineage)

Already partially documented in `07_Computational_Design/BIOMORPHIC_GROWTH_ALGORITHMS.md` (differential growth as primary candidate, Oxman/Mediated Matter Group and ICD/ITKE Stuttgart as precedent). For the shell-specific direction this week, the emphasis shifts slightly:

- Oxman's core idea we're borrowing: **material distribution follows a real performance gradient**, not decoration (e.g. Silk Pavilion's silk density follows structural/light logic, not pattern for its own sake).
- This maps directly onto the tutor's gradient requirement: if a single field (e.g. sun exposure, height, structural stress from the shell relaxation) drives both **panel overlap amount** and **rust-vs-treated finish**, we get one coherent generative system rather than two separate decisions bolted together.

## 3. Gap Between Week 3 Concepts and Current Brief

| Week 3 Concept | Primary Material | Fabric Present? | Fit to New Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gyroid Hybrid Tectonic | Oyster cages + rebar + bent sheet/mesh skin | No | Partial — skin layer is closest match, but primary structure isn't sheet metal |
| Plexus Shell | Rebar/piping + nylon rope | Partial (rope, not woven fabric) | Weak fit |
| Stigmergic Piping | Metal piping + shotcrete | No | Poor fit |

**Conclusion:** none of the three carries forward wholesale. The Gyroid skin logic (bent metal sheet + wire mesh) is the best seed to develop into a full shell system, replacing its primary oyster-cage/rebar frame with a form-found sheet-metal shell, and introducing fabric as a genuine secondary layer rather than an afterthought.

## 4. Open Questions for This Week

- Does fabric sit **inside** the shell (structural bracing/tensioning between sheet panels) or **outside** (environmental — shading, rain-screening gaps between panels)? Both are viable; affects the aggregation logic (see `02_Computational_Strategy.md`).
- What pavilion **function** justifies the shell form — gallery/enclosed space (as Week 3's Gyroid and Stigmergic concepts assumed) or a more open, beachside/threshold condition (as Plexus assumed)? Tutor's general feedback asks us to design from function/spatial quality first — needs deciding before the field-driven pattern can be meaningfully tuned.
- Rust vs. treated — see computational strategy doc for how this becomes a field output rather than a single global choice.
