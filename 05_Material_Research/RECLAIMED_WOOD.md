# Circular Wood — Material List

Started from two ideas (pallet wood, plywood) and expanded using the same categorisation logic already applied to metal in `RECLAIMED_METAL_SCRAP.md` — split by structural role first (sectional/structural stock vs. sheet/panel stock vs. found-object stock), then by specific sub-type within each role. This is deliberately not just "two materials" — wood turns out to have a genuinely wider range of reclaimed sub-categories than metal does, including a sophisticated engineered/mass-timber offcut stream that didn't show up in the first pass at all.

---

## List A — Structural/Sectional Reclaimed Timber (primary structural candidates)

Direct equivalent of the metal document's "List A — Structural/Sectional Scrap": large, load-bearing-grade timber, sourced whole rather than as offcuts.

| Type | Typical Source | Typical Dimensions | Condition Notes | Structural Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reclaimed structural beams (sawn softwood/hardwood) | Demolished buildings, barn/warehouse teardown | Large sections, highly variable — genuinely non-standard | <cite index="123-1">Structural sawn boards reclaimed from existing buildings not designed for deconstruction — condition depends entirely on prior use and how carefully deconstruction was done</cite> | Primary frame members — the timber equivalent of metal List A |
| Reclaimed glulam beams | Deconstructed mass-timber buildings, structural retrofit removals | Large engineered sections, standard-ish depths | <cite index="124-1">With design-for-deconstruction principles — mechanical fixings, modular grids, accessible connections — glulam beams can be removed intact and reinstalled elsewhere, retaining structural integrity if history is traceable and condition verifiable</cite> | High-value structural stock if sourceable — worth checking any local mass-timber deconstruction projects |
| Railway sleepers | Rail infrastructure maintenance/renewal, salvage yards | ~2.4–2.6m length, ~250×125mm section (standard) | <cite index="138-1">Generated constantly by track maintenance and renewal operations worldwide — genuinely large, heavy, weather-hardened timber sections</cite>; often creosote-treated on older sleepers — check treatment history before cutting/sanding | Heavy, dense, already weatherproofed — strong candidate for base/plinth/ground-contact structural elements given their built-in outdoor durability |

**Honesty flag:** <cite index="129-1">reused structural timber can carry hidden risk from its previous life — prior seismic/load damage, deconstruction damage, and hidden defects not visible on inspection — so any List A piece intended for genuine structural (not just decorative) use should get the same grading/testing treatment already established as this project's core strategy (see EPFL Ski Pavilion and Reclaimed Design precedents in `09_Systemic_Design_Framework/`).</cite>

## List B — Engineered Mass Timber Offcuts (genuinely new category — not in the original pallet/plywood scope)

This category didn't exist in the first pass and is the strongest single addition from expanding the research: reclaimed *engineered* wood products, not just solid sawn timber.

| Type | Typical Source | Typical Dimensions | Condition Notes | Structural Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CLT (cross-laminated timber) production offcuts | CLT manufacturing plants — rectangular cut-offs from door/window openings | <cite index="125-1">Typically around 1m×1m and 2m×1m — genuinely production waste, not damaged material</cite> | New-condition, factory offcut — the mass-timber equivalent of formply in the plywood category | <cite index="125-1">A directly precedented use: the DTC Timbershell project reuses exactly these CLT door/window cut-offs, shaping them into polygonal elements that form a wood-only shell structure</cite> — see precedent section below |
| CLST (cross-laminated secondary timber) — CLT re-manufactured from reclaimed feedstock | Specialist reuse manufacturers/researchers (UCL, Edinburgh Napier, others) | Panel-scale, matches standard CLT dimensions | <cite index="123-1">Rose et al. (2018) found no significant difference between the compressive strength and stiffness of CLST and standard CLT</cite> — genuinely structurally comparable, not a compromise material | Not yet a commercial off-the-shelf product at small scale, but worth knowing this research exists — flags that CLT-from-reclaimed-feedstock is a legitimate structural direction, not just an offcut-reuse one |
| Deconstructed CLT/glulam panels (whole, reused as panels) | Buildings originally built with design-for-deconstruction principles | Full original panel size | <cite index="129-1">Case study: the CLT Cafe in Kobe City reused shake-table-tested CLT panels — no visible damage was noted, and the panels were downcycled from primary structural use to a secondary roof structure</cite> | Best-case scenario if a local source exists — full intact panels rather than offcuts |

**Precedent — Wood ReFramed:** <cite index="125-1">a built research demonstrator featuring trusses made from reclaimed timber, where a parametric design approach distributes material according to each piece's actual structural capacity rather than producing complex geometry for its own sake — computational optimisation is used specifically to counter the unknown material properties that come with reclaimed stock.</cite> Directly the same "grade then assign" strategy already established via the EPFL Ski Pavilion and ITKE's Reclaimed Design thesis — now a third precedent confirming this is the standard academic response to reclaimed-timber variability, not a one-off idea.

**Precedent — Salvage Swings Pavilion (Somewhere Studio):** <cite index="129-1">uses reused 3-ply CLT panels</cite> — a genuinely small-scale, pavilion-appropriate example (not a full building retrofit like the Kobe or UCL cases), worth a closer look if more detail is wanted for slides.

## List C — Reclaimed Sheet/Panel Wood (dedicated category, mirrors metal's dedicated sheet list)

This is where the original "plywood" idea sits, now positioned as one of several sheet-good sub-types rather than the only one.

| Type | Typical Source | Typical Dimensions | Condition Notes | Fabrication Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formply (concrete formwork plywood) | Construction sites, concrete contractors | 2400×1200mm standard sheet, 17–19mm thick | <cite index="116-1">Generally usable only once in a concrete mould before being discarded — near-new plywood thrown away constantly on active sites</cite>; one face shows a smooth, sealed "concrete face" patina | Best-condition, highest-volume reclaimed sheet stock — should be the priority sheet-good sourcing target |
| General construction/panel offcuts | Renovation sites, joinery/cabinetmaker offcuts | Irregular, 6–18mm thickness | Widely variable condition | CNC-cut node components, connector gussets once sorted by thickness |
| Set/backdrop plywood | Theatre, event, exhibition fit-outs | Often pre-cut into usable shapes | <cite index="122-1">Substantial reusable offcuts generated once a production or event ends</cite> | Worth checking specifically for pre-curved plywood, otherwise hard to source |
| Furniture-grade/veneer-faced offcuts | Furniture manufacturers, joinery workshops | Small-to-medium, better finish | Higher-quality face veneer | Visible interior surfaces, connector detailing |
| OSB / particleboard / MDF offcuts | Construction, furniture, manufacturing waste | Sheet-scale offcuts, variable | <cite index="119-1">Contains adhesives/binders that limit recycling options compared to solid lumber, though the panels themselves remain physically reusable</cite> | Lower-visibility structural backing/blocking rather than finish surfaces |

**Honesty check (carried over from original research):** <cite index="119-1">engineered wood panels' adhesive content means these materials can be *reused* successfully but not meaningfully *recycled* back into new panel product</cite> — sourcing good-condition stock matters more here than for solid timber, which has more fallback options.

## List D — Found/Consumer-Object Reclaimed Wood (lower structural value, high character — mirrors metal's found-object list)

| Type | Typical Source | Typical Dimensions | Condition Notes | Design Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pallet deck boards | Freight/logistics pallet breakdown | ~22×95–145mm section, ~1.0–1.2m length | Softwood, nail holes, variable weathering | Kerfing/scoring for curved skin panels (Week 3's "Kerfed Skin" concept) |
| Pallet stringers/blocks | Same source — internal structural members | ~38×90mm typical | Usually sound — built to bear stacking loads | Small structural blocking, node infill |
| CHEP/rental pallet timber (blue-stamped) | Commercial logistics specifically | More dimensionally consistent than mixed pallets | Heat-treated (HT), not chemically fumigated — genuinely safer to reuse | Best-condition pallet source if sourceable — worth asking logistics companies directly |
| Wine/whisky barrel staves | Coopers, distilleries, wineries retiring barrels | Curved stave form, ~1m length, tapered width | <cite index="132-1">Staves can only be shaved/reconditioned a limited number of times before becoming too thin for barrel use — meaning a genuine, recurring waste stream exists once staves are "spent" for coopering purposes</cite> | The stave's pre-existing curvature is a genuinely useful found geometry — worth considering for any application wanting pre-curved timber without a bending process |
| Reclaimed scaffold boards | Scaffolding companies retiring boards past inspection life | Standard ~225×38mm section, ~2–3m lengths | Weathered, may have galvanised end-bands | Consistent, standardised reclaimed dimension — rare in reclaimed timber generally, worth prioritising if visual/dimensional consistency matters for a section of the pavilion |

## Cross-Category Synthesis — How Wood Now Compares to Metal and GFRC

| | Structural/Sectional (List A) | Engineered Offcuts (List B) | Sheet/Panel (List C) | Found-Object (List D) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Metal equivalent | Steel I-beams, angle iron | *(no real metal equivalent — this is wood-specific)* | Reclaimed metal sheet, ACP offcuts | License plates, cans, appliance panels |
| GFRC equivalent | Reclaimed concrete panel offcuts | *(no real equivalent)* | Fabricator panel offcuts/rejects | *(none)* |
| Wood-specific advantage | Sourced whole (beams, sleepers) rather than only as offcuts | A genuinely unique category — no metal or GFRC equivalent exists, since neither material has an "engineered composite offcut" stream at this scale | Highest-volume, most consistent reclaimed stream of all three materials (formply) | Most characterful/legible found-object stream (barrel staves' curvature, scaffold board consistency) |

**The genuinely new finding from expanding this research:** wood is the only one of the three material families with a real "engineered composite offcut" category (List B, CLT/glulam) — metal scrap has nothing structurally equivalent (metal composites like ACP are sheet-scale, not structural-scale), and GFRC's offcut stream (per `RECLAIMED_GFRC.md`) is thin and fabricator-dependent rather than a genuine industry-wide waste stream the way CLT production offcuts are becoming. This makes List B worth taking seriously as potentially the single strongest structural reclaimed-material lead across all three material families researched this semester, if a CLT fabricator relationship can be established — more promising than anything found in the metal or GFRC research.

## Fits Into the Existing System

Following the same chain format as `09_Systemic_Design_Framework/MATERIAL_FABRICATION_PRECEDENT_SYSTEM_MAP.md`:

**Chain 6 — CLT production offcuts → cutting/shaping into polygonal shell elements → DTC Timbershell project.** Strategy: solve two-dimensional reclaimed-material waste (door/window cutouts) by treating each offcut's actual boundary shape as a design input for a polygonal shell, rather than trying to force offcuts into a pre-set panel grid. Directly extends this project's growth-algorithm/paneling logic (see `06_Precedent_Research/MAIN_PRECEDENT_THIN_SHELL_METAL.md`'s stripe-segmentation lesson) to a wood-specific material stream.

**Chain 7 — Reclaimed timber (mixed grades) → parametric structural distribution → Wood ReFramed.** Strategy: identical to EPFL Ski Pavilion and Reclaimed Design (ITKE) — computational optimisation counters unknown/variable material properties by placing material where its actual (tested) capacity is needed, rather than uniformly over-specifying.

This brings the project to **seven documented chains across three material families** — the systemic research method itself is now well-established across metal, GFRC/concrete, and wood, which is worth stating plainly in the presentation as evidence the methodology has scaled properly, not just the material list.
