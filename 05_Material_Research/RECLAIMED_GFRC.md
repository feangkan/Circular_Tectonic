# Reclaimed GFRC — Material List

**Honest framing first:** unlike timber or metal, GFRC (glass fibre reinforced concrete) does not have an established public salvage/demolition-reuse stream the way timber pallets or scrap steel do — it's a relatively young material (mainstream since the 1970s–80s) mostly still in active service on the buildings it was installed in. <cite index="18-1">The research literature on GFRC/GFRP reuse is overwhelmingly about processing waste generated *during manufacturing* (offcuts, rejects, production waste), not about salvaging installed panels from demolition.</cite> So "reclaimed GFRC" for this project realistically means three different things, listed below in order of how genuinely reclaimed each one is.

## List A — Fabricator/Factory Waste (most realistic source at student scale)

| Type | Source | Typical Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Panel offcuts / trim waste | GFRC precast fabricators, facade panel manufacturers | Irregular flat/curved fragments, board-scale down to small pieces | <cite index="20-1">Established GFRC manufacturers increasingly operate zero-waste policies, reusing production waste materials internally and using technology to reduce offcuts in the first place</cite> — meaning offcut *volume* may actually be shrinking at well-run fabricators, but asking directly is still worth it |
| Rejected/failed casts | Same fabricators — panels that failed QC (colour mismatch, minor defect, damaged in demoulding) | Full or near-full panel geometry, cosmetic defects only | Structurally sound, rejected purely for appearance — potentially the single best find if a local fabricator relationship can be built |
| Mould scrap / test panels | GFRC fabricators' R&D/sample production | Small sample-sized pieces | Useful for material testing and small-scale connector/joint prototyping even if not enough volume for structure |

**Action for Melbourne sourcing:** identify 1–2 local precast/GFRC fabricators and ask directly about offcut, reject, and sample-panel availability — this is a cold-call/site-visit task, not something findable by web search alone.

## List B — Ground/Processed GFRC Waste (structural role: aggregate, not panel)

<cite index="23-1">Mechanical recycling — sorting, cutting, grinding, and screening waste GFRP/GFRC down to fine particles — is currently the most practical and lowest-impact processing route compared to thermal or chemical recycling.</cite> <cite index="26-1">Mechanical recycling also requires the least energy of any recycling method (roughly 0.27–3.03 MJ/kg).</cite>

| Type | Source | Form | Structural Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground GFRC/GFRP aggregate | Processed waste from list A, or specialist recyclers | Fine granular material | <cite index="18-1">Feasible to incorporate at low replacement proportions (research suggests up to ~20% of sand volume) into new concrete mixes for non-structural applications</cite> — i.e. this becomes an ingredient in a new cast element, not a structural panel reused whole |
| Recycled GFRP coarse aggregate | Specialist processors (cutting waste bars/panels into graded particle sizes) | Graded particle sizes | <cite index="24-1">Used to replace natural coarse aggregate in new concrete casting, with performance varying by replacement ratio</cite> |

**Relevance to this project:** if List A (whole reclaimed offcuts) doesn't yield enough volume, ground GFRC waste mixed into a new cold-cast mix (see `08_Fabrication_Methods/`) is a legitimate — and honestly documented — fallback, since it still diverts real waste material from landfill even though the final component is newly cast.

## List C — Alternative "GFRC-adjacent" Reclaimed Concrete (if true GFRC proves too hard to source)

| Type | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reclaimed precast concrete panel offcuts (non-fibre) | Demolition sites, precast yards | Already documented in `MATERIAL_RESEARCH_GUIDE.md` (Section E) — heavier and less flexible than GFRC but genuinely reclaimed via an established demolition-salvage stream |
| Reclaimed/crushed concrete as new GFRC aggregate | Demolition crushing operations | Could theoretically feed into a *new* GFRC mix as the aggregate component, combining a genuinely reclaimed material (crushed demolition concrete) with new glass fibre reinforcement — worth floating as a hybrid idea even though it's not "reclaimed GFRC" in the strict sense |

## Physical/Performance Reference Data (for whichever route is used)

- <cite index="19-1">GFRC production commonly uses recycled aggregates alongside glass fibre, sand, and cement.</cite>
- Weight: ~75% lighter than comparable solid concrete (from existing `MATERIAL_RESEARCH_GUIDE.md` research)
- <cite index="20-1">The material's malleability allows panels to be shaped smoothly around corners and edges</cite> — relevant if paired with a growth-algorithm curved surface output
- Fire performance: Class A1 (non-combustible) — a genuine advantage for an exhibition-context pavilion

## Recommendation for This Week's Direction

Given the sourcing difficulty, I'd treat GFRC as a **secondary/accent material** in the pavilion (cladding fragments, base/plinth elements, small cast connector nodes using ground waste + new resin per the cold-casting method) rather than a primary structural material — and be upfront about that reasoning in the presentation rather than overstating GFRC's availability as "reclaimed." This is also consistent with the Blue Ocean Dome precedent's material-hierarchy-by-function principle already established in Week 3's precedent research: let metal scrap (genuinely abundant, structurally provable) do the structural work, and use GFRC where its specific properties (fire rating, thin curved panels, fine surface finish) earn its place.

**See also `05_Material_Research/CIRCULAR_FIBRE_FOR_GFRC.md`** — a separate follow-up question of whether the glass fibre component itself (not just the concrete matrix or panel sourcing) can be made more circular. Basalt fibre emerges as the strongest candidate: a genuine drop-in substitute for glass fibre in the same casting process, with real architectural precedent (basalt fibre reinforced precast panels) and no change needed to the connection/casting strategy already established in `06_Precedent_Research/GFRC_PRECEDENT.md`.
