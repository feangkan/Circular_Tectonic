## 1. MATERIAL CATEGORIES & SOURCING

### A. RECLAIMED TIMBER

#### Material Properties & Grades

**Structural Capacity:**
- **Softwoods:** 10-15 MPa bending strength (typical)
- **Hardwoods:** 40-80+ MPa bending strength (durable)
- **Reclaimed advantage:** Often larger dimension lumber than new growth timber
- **Challenge:** Inconsistent quality, knots, previous nail/bolt holes

#### Sourcing in Hong Kong Context

**Primary Sources:**
1. **Demolition sites** — Old tenement buildings, industrial warehouses
   - Quality: Variable (often damaged)
   - Volume: High
   - Cost: Minimal (often removal credit)

2. **Furniture makers/carpentry shops** — Waste from production
   - Quality: Medium (production offcuts)
   - Volume: Medium
   - Cost: Low-Medium

3. **Shipping/cargo** — Wooden pallets, crates
   - Quality: High (untreated softwood)
   - Volume: Very high
   - Cost: Free-Minimal (disposal cost)

4. **Renovation contractors** — Architectural salvage
   - Quality: High (often precious hardwoods)
   - Volume: Low-Medium
   - Cost: Medium-High

#### Testing Protocol for Reclaimed Timber

**Visual Assessment:**
- [ ] Document dimensions (length, width, thickness, variation)
- [ ] Photograph grain, color, visible damage
- [ ] Mark knot locations, previous fasteners
- [ ] Note any splitting, warping, or weathering

**Structural Testing:**
- [ ] Small sample core testing for density/hardness
- [ ] Visual strength grading per local standards
- [ ] Non-destructive testing (acoustic, micro-drilling)
- [ ] Load testing on 1-2 representative pieces

**Safety Assessment:**
- [ ] Check for chemical treatment (CCA, pentachlorophenol)
- [ ] Verify no termite/rot damage (visual + tap test)
- [ ] Surface condition assessment (splinters, rough edges)

#### Application in Pavilion Design

**Structural:**
- Large dimension beams (250mm+ sections possible from reclaimed)
- Distributed load paths (multiple smaller pieces better than single large)
- Visible structural logic (expose connection strategy)

**Aesthetic:**
- Patina as design feature (don't sand out aging)
- Mix different timber types for visual interest
- Celebrate material history through expression

### B. RECYCLED/RECLAIMED PLASTIC

#### PET Plastic Bottles (Like EcoARK)

**Material Characteristics:**
- Density: 1.38 g/cm³ (lightweight)
- Tensile strength: 40-60 MPa
- Melting point: 260°C (temperature stable in normal climates)
- Color: Typically clear, blue, or green (from beverage bottles)
- Recyclable: Yes, infinite potential

#### Processing for Architectural Use

**Standard Process (EcoARK/Polli-Brick):**
1. **Collection** — Public participation or municipal waste stream
2. **Sorting** — By color, type, cleanliness
3. **Washing** — Remove labels, residue
4. **Shredding** — Into flake for processing
5. **Mold formation** — Heat + pressure into bricks
6. **Assembly** — Interlocking grooves + silicone sealant
7. **Application** — Facade panels, structural blocks

**Performance Data (Polli-Brick):**
- Weight: 50% lighter than concrete equivalent
- Fire resistance: Class A1 (fire-resistant coating)
- Thermal insulation: Moderate (air pockets help)
- Durability: 20+ years tested in Taiwan (typhoon climate)
- UV resistance: Requires protective coating

#### Sourcing PET Bottles in Hong Kong

**Hong Kong Context:**
- Population: ~7.5 million
- Annual PET consumption: ~500+ million bottles
- Current recycling rate: ~25%
- Abundance: Very high
- Organizational opportunity: Partnerships with recyclers (Swire Properties, Asia Waste Management)

**Logistics:**
- Collection: Coordinate with recycling centers
- Volume for 1,925m² pavilion: ~500,000-1,000,000 bottles (if used as primary material)
- Cost: Essentially free (waste diversion value)
- Processing: Partner with plastic fabricators or commission custom molding

#### Testing Protocol for Recycled Plastic

**Material Properties:**
- [ ] Tensile testing on virgin plastic (control)
- [ ] Tensile testing on recycled material (recycled can be 5-10% weaker)
- [ ] Color consistency across batch
- [ ] Thermal stability testing
- [ ] Durability under UV exposure (1000+ hour weathering)
- [ ] Fire testing per standards

**Assembled System Testing:**
- [ ] Interlocking performance (assembly/disassembly cycles)
- [ ] Joint strength (silicone sealant durability)
- [ ] Weather exposure (rain infiltration, wind load)
- [ ] Thermal cycling (-5°C to +60°C typical range)

### C. GFRC (GLASS FIBER REINFORCED CONCRETE)

#### Material Composition & Properties

**Typical Mix:**
- Portland cement: 40%
- Fine sand: 35%
- Glass fibers (alkali-resistant): 5-10%
- Water: ~20%
- Additives (polymers, pigments): 2-5%

**Performance Characteristics:**
- <cite index="45-1">Characterized by enhanced tensile, flexural, and splitting strength; impact, abrasion, spalling, fire, and freeze-thaw resistance as well as ductility, toughness, and permeability</cite>
- Weight: <cite index="42-1">75% lighter than comparable concrete products</cite>
- Thickness: Typically 0.5–0.75" (12–19mm)
- Tensile strength: 6–12 MPa (much higher than plain concrete)
- Flexural strength: 10–18 MPa
- Fire rating: Class A1 (non-combustible)

#### GFRC Fabrication Methods

**Spray-up (Most Common for Facades):**
1. Prepare mold (typically fiberglass or steel)
2. Spray chopped glass fibers + cement slurry onto surface
3. Compact with rollers
4. Cure (typically 24–48 hours)
5. Release from mold
6. Final finishing (grinding, painting, sealing)

**Hand-Lay-Up:**
- Labor-intensive but allows precise control
- Better for thin sections, complex geometries
- Higher quality finish possible
- More expensive

**Casting (Block/Precast):**
- For architectural elements (columns, decorative panels)
- Allows color variation through layering
- Reproducible units

#### Design Considerations for GFRC

**Advantages:**
- <cite index="41-1">Lightweight nature makes installation easier and can reduce overall weight of structure</cite>
- Complex shapes possible (curved facades, ornamental elements)
- Thin sections reduce material use
- Can mimic natural materials (limestone, stone, wood texture)
- <cite index="43-1">No need for protective concrete cover thickness since glass fibers cannot rust</cite>
- Color options through pigment addition

**Limitations:**
- Tensile strength depends on fiber orientation (directional weakness possible)
- Shrinkage during curing (requires careful joint detailing)
- Cost: More expensive than plain concrete (30–50% premium)
- Requires skilled fabrication
- Long-term performance still being monitored (40+ year data limited)

#### GFRC Testing & Quality Control

**Fabrication QC:**
- [ ] Fiber distribution uniformity (visual inspection)
- [ ] Fiber length verification (typically 12–50mm)
- [ ] Cure temperature/humidity monitoring
- [ ] Surface finish inspection (cracks, delamination)
- [ ] Color batch consistency

**Performance Testing:**
- [ ] Tensile testing per ASTM C1558
- [ ] Flexural testing per ASTM C1161
- [ ] Impact resistance testing
- [ ] Freeze-thaw cycling per ASTM C666
- [ ] Water absorption per ASTM C948
- [ ] Fire rating per ASTM E84 (Flame Spread, Smoke Development)

#### GFRC in Hong Kong Context

**Fabricators Available:**
- Several international GFRC suppliers operate in Asia
- Local concrete precast companies can develop GFRC capability
- Cost: ~HK$500–1,500 per m² for basic panels (depending on complexity)

**Climate Considerations:**
- High humidity: Accelerates weathering (protective sealers needed)
- Typhoons: Excellent wind resistance possible
- UV exposure: Requires UV-protective coating
- Salt spray (if coastal): Protective coatings critical

**Best Applications for Pavilion:**
- Facade cladding (lightweight, weather-resistant)
- Decorative architectural elements
- Not ideal for primary structure (use timber or steel instead)
- Partner material with reclaimed timber or steel frame

### D. RECLAIMED STRUCTURAL STEEL

#### Sources & Characteristics

**Typical Reclaimed Steel Elements:**
- I-beams, H-beams (from buildings)
- Angle iron and flat stock
- Structural tubing (square, rectangular, round)
- Steel plates
- **Advantage:** Zero degradation from previous use (unlike concrete)
- **Advantage:** Fully recyclable with zero quality loss

#### Design for Reclaimed Steel

**Advantages:**
- Proven performance history (building it survived)
- Can span large distances
- Connection logic easily visible
- Rust patina can be design feature
- Smaller sections than timber for equivalent strength

**Limitations:**
- Highly variable sizing (non-standard dimensions)
- Surface rust requires cleaning/treatment
- Previous bolt holes may need welding or re-drilling
- Cost: Similar to new steel (material cost) + labor for cleaning/prep

#### Protection Strategy

**For Pavilion (Temporary, 5–10 year life):**
1. **Wire brush** to remove loose rust
2. **Rust converter** chemical (converts rust to protective coating)
3. **Rust-inhibiting primer** (2 coats)
4. **Topcoat paint** (2 coats) in selected color
5. **Annual inspection** + touch-up paint

**For Permanent Installation:**
- Hot-dip galvanizing (most durable)
- Sherardizing (alternative)
- Paint systems: 3–4 coats total thickness
- Consider weathering steel (Corten) for aesthetic patina control

#### Connection Strategy with Reclaimed Steel

**Bolted Connections (Preferred for Circular Design):**
- Allows disassembly/relocation
- Can use oversized bolt holes if previous holes don't align
- Torque spec: Follow AISC standards
- Cost: Bolts + labor for tightening/inspection

**Welded Connections (Permanent):**
- Stronger, more rigid
- Requires certified welding
- Difficult to disassemble
- Potential heat damage to adjacent paint/materials

**Hybrid (Recommended):**
- Welded primary connections (for strength)
- Bolted secondary connections (for assembly/disassembly)
- Allows on-site flexibility

### E. RECLAIMED CONCRETE PANELS

#### Sourcing & Preparation

**Typical Sources:**
- Demolished building floor slabs
- Precast parking structures
- Industrial platform systems
- Bridge decking (sometimes)

**Conditions Typically Found:**
- Relatively good structural integrity
- Surface weathering, staining, minor cracks
- Previous reinforcement (if steel, hazard during cutting)
- Irregular underside (if cast)

#### Preparation & Cutting

**Cleaning:**
- Pressure wash to remove surface dirt
- Wire brush to remove loose material
- Inspect for asbestos (older buildings) — mandatory testing before handling

**Cutting/Sizing:**
- Diamond wire cutting (precise, minimal waste)
- Wet saw cutting (slower, good surface finish)
- Cost: HK$100–500 per cut depending on panel size
- Plan cuts carefully to minimize waste

#### Design Applications

**As Structural Element:**
- Load-bearing walls (multiple courses)
- Roof systems (if properly supported)
- Flooring (if sound)

**As Aesthetic Cladding:**
- Exterior wall panels
- Interior accent walls
- Weather face shows aggregate, color variation (historical patina)

#### Connection Strategy for Concrete Panels

<cite index="35-1">Post-installed steel anchors enable reversible connections, allowing pavilions to be dismantled and reassembled in different locations</cite>

**System Components:**
1. **Main structure** (steel or timber frame)
2. **Angle brackets** bolted to frame
3. **Post-installed anchors** (drilled into concrete panel edges)
4. **Connections** bolted between brackets + anchors
5. **Allows** — disassembly by unbolting

**Joint Detailing:**
- Mortar joints (if vertical stacking) or
- Sealant joints (if panels frame-mounted)
- Flashing at top edge (weather protection)
- Drainage consideration (sloped surfaces, weep holes)

#### Testing for Reclaimed Concrete

**Before Reuse:**
- [ ] Visual inspection for cracks (depth evaluation)
- [ ] Soundness testing (tap test for delamination)
- [ ] Bearing capacity verification (load test sample)
- [ ] Environmental testing (if from industrial source)
- [ ] Surface analysis (coating composition, contamination)

### F. PAPER TUBES (SHIGERU BAN METHOD)

#### Material Specifications

**Standard Paper Tube Dimensions:**
- Diameter: 100–300mm typical
- Wall thickness: 8–15mm (laminated kraft paper)
- Length: Up to 40m possible (Expo 2000 used 40m tubes)
- Weight: ~2 kg/m (very lightweight)
- Load capacity: 80–150 kN compression per tube (depending on diameter/thickness)

**Composition:**
- Recycled kraft paper (60–80%)
- Polyester tape wrapping (provides tensile strength)
- Lamination adhesive
- Interior wax coating (moisture resistance)

#### Structural Properties

**Advantages:**
- Very lightweight (enables large spans with minimal support)
- Recyclable (can be composted or recycled)
- Demountable (friction fit, bolted connections)
- Repeatable unit (standardization)
- Aesthetic: Shows material honestly

**Limitations:**
- Moisture sensitivity (requires protective coatings)
- Long-term performance still being monitored (30+ years)
- Weather protection critical (especially in humid HK climate)
- Connection complexity (requires precise fitting)
- Cost: Mid-range (more than standard steel, less than specialty materials)

#### Environmental Specifications for HK

**Climate Challenges:**
- High humidity (90%+ common)
- Typhoons (140+ km/h winds possible)
- Tropical rainfall (annual 2,400mm)
- Salt spray (if coastal location)

**Protection Strategy:**
1. **Base coating** — water-resistant paint (2 coats)
2. **UV protection** — polyurethane topcoat
3. **Moisture barrier** — silicone sealant at all connections
4. **Drainage** — angled surfaces, weep holes at base
5. **Ventilation** — air circulation to prevent condensation
6. **Inspection schedule** — monthly during wet season

#### Connection Design for Paper Tubes

**Friction Fit (Simple):**
- Telescoping tubes (larger diameter slides over smaller)
- Requires precision tolerance (±5mm)
- No fasteners visible
- Very clean aesthetic
- Challenge: Precise manufacturing needed

**Bolted Connectors:**
- Custom steel or aluminum connector plates
- Bolts through paper tubes (requires reinforced holes)
- Allows angular connections (not just axial)
- Easy to inspect/tighten
- More visible connection

**3D-Printed Connectors (Innovation):**
- Custom fit to non-standard dimensions
- Accommodates slight manufacturing variances
- Reusable design library
- Emerging technology (test on small scale first)

## 2. MATERIAL COMBINATION STRATEGIES

### Timber + Steel Hybrid

**When to Use:**
- Timber for primary structure (spans, dramatic forms)
- Steel for connections (precision, durability)
- Steel for concentrated loads (points, cantilevers)

**Advantages:**
- Timber carries loads efficiently (lower embodied carbon per kN span)
- Steel provides connection precision (accommodates non-standard timber)
- Visual expression: Timber warmth + steel precision
- Disassembly: Bolted steel connections allow relocation

**Design Example:**
- Large timber beams (300mm depth)
- Steel connection plates (bolted)
- Custom 3D-printed connectors at joints
- Thermal expansion management (different materials move differently)

### GFRC Facade + Timber Frame

**When to Use:**
- Lightweight facade aesthetic
- Thermal insulation needed (GFRC has better properties than concrete)
- Complex shaped facade (GFRC can achieve this, timber frame can't)

**Advantages:**
- GFRC: Lightweight, weather-resistant, shapeable
- Timber: Structural span, material warmth
- Separation of functions: Structure vs. skin
- Disassembly: GFRC panels bolted to frame

**Design Example:**
- Timber post/beam frame (primary structure)
- GFRC panel cladding (secondary, thermal break)
- Bolted connection system
- Cavity for insulation between frame and cladding

### Reclaimed Plastic Blocks + Steel Frame

**When to Use:**
- Large facade area (economies of scale)
- Translucent aesthetic desired
- Integration of recycling message

**Advantages:**
- Material tells story (people recognize bottles)
- Lightweight system
- Modular assembly (LEGO-like interlocking)
- Can include color variety (different bottle colors)

**Design Example:**
- Steel frame (structural support)
- Polli-Brick modules (facade system, interlocking)
- Minimal fastening needed (friction fit + silicone)
- Interior finish: Visible bottle bottoms create interesting light effect

## 4. TESTING PROTOCOLS FOR YOUR STUDIO

### Week-by-Week Testing Schedule

**Week 1–2 (HK Phase):**
- [ ] Collect material samples from identified sources
- [ ] Document with photography (scale reference, color, texture)
- [ ] Basic assessment (dimensions, condition, obvious defects)
- [ ] Arrange for small-scale lab testing (if accessible)

**Weeks 3–6 (Melbourne Phase):**
- [ ] Structural testing of key materials (sample load tests)
- [ ] Connection prototyping with actual materials
- [ ] 3D printing of connector samples
- [ ] Assembly/disassembly trials

**Weeks 7–12 (Melbourne Phase):**
- [ ] Finalize material specifications
- [ ] Test refined connection details
- [ ] Validate performance against requirements
- [ ] Document findings

### Testing Checklist by Material

**For Each Material Tested:**

**Visual/Tactile Assessment:**
- [ ] Photograph (good lighting, color reference card)
- [ ] Dimensional accuracy (measure 3+ samples)
- [ ] Surface condition (note defects, patina, texture)
- [ ] Uniformity (variation within batch/lot)
- [ ] Smell/off-gassing (sealed plastic, treated wood)

**Structural Assessment:**
- [ ] Strength testing (if lab access available)
- [ ] Durability assessment (visual grain, cracks, rot)
- [ ] Workability (can it be cut, drilled, joined?)
- [ ] Weight (compare to specification)

**Weather/Durability:**
- [ ] UV exposure (if material sensitive)
- [ ] Water absorption (relevant for organic materials)
- [ ] Thermal movement (expansion/contraction)
- [ ] Weathering characteristics (will it age well?)

**Connection Testing:**
- [ ] Mock-up of 1–2 typical connections
- [ ] Bolt-tightening protocol verification
- [ ] Repeated assembly/disassembly cycles
- [ ] Load testing of connection (if possible)

## 5. COST ESTIMATION BY MATERIAL

### Typical Hong Kong Costs (2026 Estimates)

| Material | Cost per Unit | Source | Notes |
|----------|---------------|--------|-------|
| Reclaimed timber (structural) | HK$50–200/m³ | Demolition | Highly variable by quality |
| Reclaimed lumber (rough) | HK$200–500/m³ | Sawmills | May require milling |
| PET bottles (clean, sorted) | HK$0.50–1 per bottle | Recyclers | Free if arranged direct |
| GFRC panels (custom made) | HK$800–2,000/m² | Fabricators | Includes labor, mold |
| Reclaimed steel (I-beams, etc) | HK$500–1,200/tonne | Scrap yards | Similar to new steel |
| Paper tubes (standard sizes) | HK$100–300 per tube | Specialty suppliers | Import available |
| Steel plate/angle (new) | HK$8–15/kg | Suppliers | For connections |
| 3D-printed connectors | HK$50–200 per piece | 3D print services | Depends on complexity |

### Cost Optimization Strategies

1. **Source locally** — Eliminates import costs, supports local recycling
2. **Negotiate volume discounts** — Use entire material batch consistently
3. **Partner with material suppliers** — Case studies / portfolio value = discount
4. **Minimize waste** — Every cutting plan matters
5. **Consider assembly labor** — Simpler assembly (fewer parts) = lower cost
