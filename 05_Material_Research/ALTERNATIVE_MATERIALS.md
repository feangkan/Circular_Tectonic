# Alternative Material Options — Suggested

Six alternatives, chosen specifically to complement the metal scrap + GFRC direction and the growth-algorithm/biomimicry lead — not a generic re-run of the Week 3 timber/PET list. Each is picked because it either (a) solves a specific gap the metal+GFRC pairing leaves open, or (b) reinforces the "natural mimicry" concept at the material level, not just the geometry level.

---

## 1. Reclaimed Wire Mesh / Chain-Link / Expanded Metal

**Source:** Demolition fencing, construction site security mesh, expanded metal offcuts from fabricators.
**Why suggested:** A growth-algorithm network is fundamentally a mesh/lattice logic — wire mesh is the one reclaimed material that's *already* the right topology before any processing. Chain-link specifically can be cut, reshaped, and re-tensioned by hand into doubly-curved surfaces, which most rigid sheet materials can't do without specialist tooling.
**Fit:** Skin/infill layer over a metal-scrap primary frame; could also be woven into a growth-pattern-informed screen rather than a flat panel.
**Caution:** Not structural on its own — always a secondary/infill material.

## 2. Reclaimed Steel Cable / Wire Rope

**Source:** Elevator/crane maintenance offcuts, marine/dockyard salvage, decommissioned playground or signage rigging.
**Why suggested:** Tension elements are the natural counterpart to a growth-network's compression members (branches need "roots" holding them in tension) — and cable is one of very few reclaimed materials that performs *better* the more irregular/kinked it already is, since pre-existing bend memory can be worked with rather than fought.
**Fit:** Tension bracing between growth-network nodes; could also translate a differential-growth curve pattern directly into a woven/draped cable net.
**Caution:** Requires proper end-fitting/termination hardware (swages, thimbles) for safety — not a place to improvise connections.

## 3. Mycelium Composite (grown, not reclaimed — included deliberately)

**Source:** Grown in-house from agricultural waste substrate (sawdust, straw) + mushroom mycelium — genuinely "manufactured" from waste rather than found, so flagged honestly as a different category from the reclaimed materials above.
**Why suggested:** This is the most direct material-level expression of "natural mimicry" available — <cite index="46-1">bio-utilization approaches like this co-opt an actual living biological process rather than simulating one computationally</cite>, which pairs conceptually with using a differential-growth *algorithm* elsewhere in the same project. Growing a component is the literal, non-metaphorical version of "growth over assembly."
**Fit:** Lightweight infill panels or a small growable connector/node component — genuinely low-tech and studio-achievable (no fabrication facility needed, just substrate, spores, and a mould, over roughly 1–2 weeks of growth time).
**Caution:** Needs to be fully dried/heat-treated to halt growth before use, and kept away from ongoing moisture exposure — a real constraint for Hong Kong's humidity if this material is carried through to the HK phase.

## 4. Reclaimed Glass (cullet or panel offcuts)

**Source:** Glazier/window fabricator offcuts, broken tempered glass from renovation sites, bottle-glass cullet from recycling streams.
**Why suggested:** Pairs naturally with the GFRC direction (glass fibre is already in the material system) and offers a translucency the metal scrap can't — useful if the growth-network geometry benefits from a light-filtering skin the way several precedents already researched (Komorebi Pavilion, MIT's 3D-printed recycled glass bricks) demonstrate.
**Fit:** Small-scale infill between metal-scrap frame members, or crushed/cullet form as decorative aggregate in a GFRC or cold-cast connector mix.
**Caution:** Tempered glass shatters into small pieces when broken (safer to handle than annealed glass shards) but still needs careful edge-finishing for any exposed application.

## 5. Reclaimed Rubber (conveyor belt offcuts, tyre rubber)

**Source:** Industrial conveyor belt replacement offcuts, tyre recyclers.
**Why suggested:** Offers something none of the rigid materials above can — flexible, weatherproof, self-damping joints. A growth-network structure with many small variable-angle connections could genuinely benefit from a flexible gasket/washer material at nodes, reducing the precision each individual joint needs to achieve.
**Fit:** Node gaskets/washers between metal scrap members (reduces reliance on perfectly matched connector geometry); could also be a ground-plane surface material.
**Caution:** UV degradation over years of exposure — fine for a temporary studio pavilion, worth flagging as a limitation if it were ever proposed for the permanent HK installation.

## 6. Reclaimed Bicycle Components (frames, spokes, chains)

**Source:** Bike shops (constant stream of scrapped frames/wheels from repairs), council bike-share decommissioning, cycling co-ops.
**Why suggested:** An underused but genuinely abundant urban waste stream, and bicycle spokes in particular are pre-tensioned thin steel wire — a small-scale analogue to the cable-tension idea above, but light enough to prototype connector/node details by hand before committing to full-scale steel.
**Fit:** Small tension elements, decorative screen infill, or literal prototyping material for testing growth-network node geometry at a manageable scale before scaling up to structural steel.
**Caution:** Mixed alloys across different frame ages/brands (steel vs. aluminium vs. occasionally carbon fibre) — sort before any welding, same caution as List C in the metal scrap document.

## Summary — How These Six Fit the Overall Material Hierarchy

| Material | Structural Role | Ties To |
|---|---|---|
| Wire mesh/chain-link | Infill/skin | Growth-network topology directly |
| Steel cable | Tension bracing | Growth-network's implied tension/compression logic |
| Mycelium composite | Light infill/grown node | Literal expression of "growth" concept |
| Reclaimed glass | Translucent infill | GFRC's glass-fibre link; light-filtering precedents |
| Reclaimed rubber | Flexible node gasket | Tolerance for growth-network's inherent geometric variability |
| Bicycle components | Small tension elements / prototyping | Low-cost, hand-scale testing before full steel commitment |

None of these are meant to replace metal scrap or GFRC as the primary materials — they're gap-fillers for roles metal/GFRC don't cover well (translucency, tension, flexibility, literal growth), chosen so the material palette matches the different structural roles a growth-algorithm network actually needs.
