## 4. KEY PRECEDENT PAVILIONS — RANKED BY RELEVANCE TO YOUR STUDIO

### 🏆 **Tier 1: Most Relevant**

**EcoARK Pavilion (Taiwan, 2010)**
- **Why:** Proof that massive scale possible with recycled material
- **Material:** 1.5M PET bottles → Polli-Brick system
- **Scale:** 9-story, 1,925 m²
- **Key Learning:** Material modularity, interlocking logic, contextual response
- **Link:** https://www.miniwiz.com/en/miniwiz-project/ecoark

**Circular Pavilion (Paris, 2015)**
- **Why:** Shows reclaimed materials as design expression, not hidden
- **Material:** 180 reclaimed timber doors + waste furniture
- **Scale:** Medium temporary pavilion
- **Key Learning:** Material-driven façade, circulation through collections
- **Link:** https://www.encoreheureux.com/

**Blue Ocean Dome (Japan, 2025)** ⭐ MOST CURRENT
- **Why:** Contemporary application, material hierarchy, relocatable
- **Material:** Paper tubes + bamboo + CFRP (hybrid system)
- **Scale:** 3 domes, 1,925 m²
- **Key Learning:** Material selection by function, temporary/permanent strategy
- **Link:** https://www.shigerubanarchitects.com/

## 5. DESIGN APPROACHES — QUICK METHODS

### **Approach 1: Material-Driven Design**
1. Catalogue available materials
2. Identify constraints & opportunities
3. Generate forms from material properties
4. Develop expression through material logic

*Best for:* Contextual, locally-sourced projects (your Hong Kong studio)

### **Approach 2: Design-for-Disassembly (DfD)**
1. Design reversible connections
2. Standardize assembly logic
3. Create clear sequencing
4. Enable relocation/reuse

*Best for:* Temporary pavilions, future adaptability

### **Approach 3: Hybrid Material Systems**
1. Identify primary material
2. Select complementary material for performance
3. Design connections between materials
4. Leverage each material's strengths

*Best for:* Achieving performance + aesthetics simultaneously

### **Approach 4: Adaptive Reuse**
1. Assess existing structure/materials
2. Program new use
3. Design transformation
4. Express history through material retention

*Best for:* Site-responsive, heritage-conscious projects

## 7. HONG KONG CONTEXT CONSIDERATIONS

### Potential Material Sources
- **Demolition sites:** Reclaimed timber, concrete, steel
- **Industrial waste:** Plastic, metals, composites
- **Farm regions:** Natural fibers (wool, straw, bamboo)
- **Urban salvage networks:** Doors, windows, fixtures
- **Port/shipping:** Containers, pallets, logistics materials

### Climate/Context Factors
- Typhoon resistance required (lightweight modular systems ideal)
- High humidity (durability critical, drainage important)
- Density/cost (salvage reduces material costs significantly)
- Local craftsmanship (hand assembly possible with small teams)

## 10. QUICK QUESTIONS FOR ANALYSIS

When studying a precedent, ask:

1. **Material:** What is the primary material? Where did it come from?
2. **Constraint:** What was the material limitation? How did it shape the design?
3. **Innovation:** What's never been done before with this material?
4. **Assembly:** How do pieces connect? Is it reversible?
5. **Context:** Why this material for this place? What problem does it address?
6. **Aesthetic:** How does the material express the design intent?
7. **Durability:** How will it age? Will it improve or degrade?
8. **Reuse:** Can it be disassembled and relocated? Recycled?
9. **Cost:** What's the economic advantage of reuse?
10. **Scale:** Could this approach scale to larger projects?
