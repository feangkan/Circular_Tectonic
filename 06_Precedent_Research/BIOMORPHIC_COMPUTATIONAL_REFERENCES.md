### 2. Assemble Studio (London) — Community-Led Material Reuse

**Who:** Turner Prize-winning (2015) architecture/art collective, London.

**Key Project — Granby Four Streets, Liverpool:**
<cite index="90-1">Assemble worked alongside a resilient local community to reimagine a post-industrial area, restoring homes rather than demolishing them, bringing back historic value while incorporating modern elements</cite>. <cite index="92-1">The "enrichment program" saw members craft missing hardware such as doorknobs by hand, while training neighbours in refurbishment skills to sustain the project</cite>.

**Design Philosophy:**
<cite index="94-1">"You're not trying to start things from scratch" — an approach that prioritises existing communities, materials, and networks, working with residents' own incremental interventions rather than imposing a masterplan</cite>.

**Relevance to Circular Tectonics:**
✓ Community-embedded material reuse (contrast to top-down "sustainable" design)
✓ Craft/hand-fabrication as legitimate response to material scarcity
✓ Social process as inseparable from material process — relevant to your CUHK collaboration
✓ Granby Workshop: turned salvaged/waste materials into sellable products (tiles, etc.) — a circular economy business model, not just a design gesture

**Link:** https://www.dezeen.com/tag/assemble/

### 3. Rotor / Rotor Deconstruction (Brussels) — Material Reuse Infrastructure

**Who:** <cite index="81-1">Brussels-based research and design practice founded 2006 investigating "the organization of the material environment"; in 2016 launched Rotor Deconstruction, a cooperative organizing reuse of construction materials through dismantling, processing, and trading of salvaged building components</cite>.

**Key Project — Brouckère Tower, Brussels:**
<cite index="82-1">Over five months, the building's blue limestone façade was carefully removed slab by slab — 280 square metres, 140 tonnes of stone — to be reused in interior and exterior renovations</cite>.

**Business Model Innovation:**
<cite index="87-1">Rotor Deconstruction dismantles, processes, and trades salvaged building components with a focus on finishing materials — hardware, doors, flooring, furniture, lighting fixtures — while maintaining strict documentation of ownership and only engaging projects with proper demolition permits</cite>.

**Founder's Position:**
<cite index="83-1">Co-founder Maarten Gielen notes that when Rotor started ~15 years ago, sustainability was seen mostly as insulation/glazing performance — "totally absurd, focusing only on energy consumption levels after the delivery of the building, when the energy used in making it was not even looked at"</cite>.

**Relevance to Circular Tectonics:**
✓ Directly maps to your studio's "material cataloguing → classification → design criteria" pipeline
✓ Rotor's **opalis.eu** database of reuse dealers is a model for a digital material catalogue
✓ Legal/documentation rigor (ownership, permits) — a practical lesson for your HK material sourcing
✓ Distinction between the *design practice* (Rotor) and the *material supply cooperative* (RotorDC) — useful model for how "material research" and "design proposal" can be organizationally separate but linked

**Link:** https://rotordc.com/ | https://rotordb.org/

### 4. Lacaton & Vassal — "Never Demolish" Philosophy

**Who:** Anne Lacaton & Jean-Philippe Vassal, Pritzker Prize 2021 (France).

**Core Philosophy:** *"Never demolish, never remove or replace, always add, transform, and reuse"* — their most famous stated principle, developed across large-scale social housing retrofits (e.g., Grand Parc Bordeaux, Tour Bois-le-Prêtre).

**Note for further research:** I did not deep-dive this practice with fresh searches this round (given scope), but flag it as **essential reading** — their work is the clearest built precedent for "reclaimed structure as the generator of the new design" at building (not pavilion) scale. Suggested starting points:
- Pritzker Prize citation/laureate page (pritzkerprize.com)
- "Freedom of Use" (Lacaton & Vassal's published manifesto)

**Relevance:** Directly informs the studio's core provocation — reclaimed material/structure as **generator**, not constraint.

### 5. The Living (David Benjamin) — Bio-Computation & Material Research

**Who:** <cite index="97-1">David Benjamin, Founding Principal, Associate Professor at Columbia GSAPP; work combines research and practice through prototyping, focused on the intersection of biology, computation, and design, articulated through three frameworks: bio-processing, bio-sensing, and bio-manufacturing</cite>. **Other work:** <cite index="93-1">Multimaterial 3D printing of structures based on bacterial forms, using bacteria to create building materials, environmental sensors embedded in building envelopes</cite>.

**Link:** https://www.thelivingnewyork.com/

### 9. Neri Oxman & The Mediated Matter Group (MIT Media Lab) — "Material Ecology"

**Core Concept:**
<cite index="40-1">Oxman coined "material ecology" to describe bringing together materials science, digital fabrication, and organic design to produce techniques and objects informed by the structural, systemic, and aesthetic wisdom of nature — integrating computational form-generation with research of natural phenomena, operating at the intersection of biology, engineering, materials science, and computer science</cite>.

**The "Growth Over Assembly" Principle:**
<cite index="41-1">Material ecology describes "an emerging field in which humans, automated processes, and nature unite to produce objects and structures that are designed through growth, with no assembly required"</cite>.

**9-Point Design Philosophy** (directly useful as a conceptual framework for your Concept Pin-Up):
<cite index="47-1">Nature as client; growth over assembly; integration over segregation; difference over repetition; decay over disposal; activist design; system over object; non-human-centered design; technology over typology</cite>.

**Key Projects to Study:**
- **Silk Pavilion** — <cite index="42-1">a cocoon-like dome digitally co-fabricated with 6,500 silkworms</cite>
- **Aguahoja** — <cite index="47-1">a catalogue of material experiments spanning four years, showing the range of aesthetics and behaviours achieved in medium-to-large-scale 3D prints via performative geometric toolpaths, generative design, bio-composite distributions, and variable fabrication parameters</cite>
- **Ocean Pavilion / Glass Pavilion** — <cite index="42-1">demonstrating large-scale robotic fabrication of crustacean-derived materials</cite>
- **Synthetic Apiary** — co-fabrication with bees
- **Vespers** — <cite index="45-1">multi-material 3D-printed death masks using a Stratasys Objet500 Connex3 printer</cite>

**Reading:**
- Book: *Mediated Matter* (MIT Press) — <cite index="43-1">the first comprehensive overview of Oxman's design work across two decades</cite>
- Exhibition catalogue: *Neri Oxman: Material Ecology* (MoMA, 2020) — <cite index="48-1">features essays by Paola Antonelli and Hadas A. Steiner</cite>

**Relevance to Circular Tectonics:**
✓ "Growth over assembly" is a genuinely different paradigm from all the reclaimed-material precedents you've studied so far — worth positioning your own proposal *against* or *alongside* this idea
✓ Bio-composite material distribution via computational toolpaths = advanced version of your "material-informed form generation" brief requirement
✓ Consider: could a connector or infill component in your 3×3×3m pavilion be **grown** (mycelium, bio-composite) rather than reclaimed or printed?

**Link:** https://oxman.com/ | https://www.media.mit.edu/groups/mediated-matter/overview/

### 10. ICD/ITKE Research Pavilions — University of Stuttgart (Biomimetic Computation)

**Who:** Institute for Computational Design and Construction (ICD, Prof. Achim Menges) + Institute of Building Structures and Structural Design (ITKE, Prof. Jan Knippers), University of Stuttgart. An annual series of full-scale research pavilions since 2010, each testing a biomimetic principle through a novel robotic fabrication process.

**Methodology (applies across the whole series):**
<cite index="53-1">All research pavilions are based on a design methodology employing computational processes of morphogenesis to synthesise multiple design criteria — fabrication process, material behavior, structural performance — into architectural form, developed via an integrative computational design tool</cite>.

**Key Pavilions:**

**2012 — Lobster Cuticle Pavilion**
<cite index="50-1">Entirely robotically fabricated from carbon and glass fibre composites, investigating the material and morphological principles of arthropod exoskeletons through filament winding; despite its size, the semi-transparent skin weighed less than 320kg, with the structural logic revealed through the spatial arrangement of fibres</cite>. <cite index="51-1">A closed digital information chain linked the project's model, finite element simulations, material testing, and robot control</cite>.

**2013–14 — Fibre Composite Shells**
<cite index="49-1">A parallel bottom-up design strategy for biomimetic investigation of natural fiber composite shells, developing a winding technique for modular, double-layered fiber composite structures that minimises formwork while maintaining geometric freedom</cite>.

**2015–16 — BUGA Fibre Pavilion (Segmented Timber Shells)**
<cite index="52-1">A bottom-up strategy based on biomimetic investigation of natural segmented plate structures and novel robotic fabrication methods for sewing thin layers of plywood — the first pavilion to employ industrial sewing of wood elements at architectural scale</cite>.

**2016–17 — Long-Span Fibre Composite**
<cite index="54-1">Building-scale fabrication of glass and carbon fibre-reinforced composites, created by laying a combined total of 184 km of resin-impregnated glass and carbon fibre</cite>.

**Relevance to Circular Tectonics:**
✓ **Directly relevant methodology transfer**: their "integrative computational design tool" synthesising fabrication + material behaviour + structural performance = exactly what your Phase 2 (Weeks 4–8) computational workflow should aim for, just applied to reclaimed rather than composite materials
✓ Multi-disciplinary team model (architects + engineers + biologists + palaeontologists) — worth citing as precedent for RMIT-CUHK collaboration structure
✓ **Timber sewing technique (2015-16)** is highly relevant if you work with reclaimed thin timber/plywood — a joining method with zero additional hardware

**Link:** https://www.icd.uni-stuttgart.de/projects/ | https://www.itke.uni-stuttgart.de/research/icd-itke-research-pavilions/
